"Do you think you’re so much better than those people?”

I couldn’t help but think, yes.

I poke at people, you know? I like it. Prodding at morals. What is the edge of your comfort, what’s your tipping point? Why? Somehow, it always leads to them getting unhappy with me. I wonder what’s up with that. Once in middle school, I was proposing trolley problems with my advisor.

Imagine you’re a doctor. You have one perfectly healthy patient. And 5 who are each missing an organ. If you take a heart, lung, bladder, whatever, from the healthy one and give each of them to your dying patients, there is a 100% chance they will live, but the one (formerly) healthy patient also has a 100% chance of dying. Do you do it?

She said no. She’d definitely lose her medical license, and she’d be able to help more people if she kept it. I nodded sagely, “Then what if you wouldn’t lose your license? Nobody would find out, would you do it then?” “How about if there were more people? If you could save 7 with just the death of one?” “How about 10?” “What if the healthy patient was a bad person?” “What if the dying were scientists who were on the verge of discovering the cure to cancer?” She quickly became annoyed with me. “You’re just trying to get me to say yes!” I remember her scowling, and I couldn’t help but think, ‘Isn’t that the point? To see where your line in the sand is?’
